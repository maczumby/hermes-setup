Part 2

Pick the brain

The agent and the model are separate things. That's one of the reasons to own your own agent: you pick which model powers it, and you can change your mind later without losing anything.

1. Log it into a subscription you already pay for

Out of the box the agent charges work against prepaid credit, and one long job can quietly drain it. The safe move for today: log the agent into a subscription you already have, so the work runs on subscription tokens instead of a per-use bill.

Our recommendation is ChatGPT ($20 Plus) — the agent logs into your account and runs on Codex, and it's the path we've tested most in these workshops. GitHub Copilot and Grok subscriptions also work if that's what you have.

Two things that trip people up: a Claude subscription does not work for Hermes login. That's a terms-of-service thing, not a bug. And if you use a raw API key instead of a subscription, set a hard spending cap at the provider first and don't give the agent any scheduled jobs until you've watched a day of costs.

In the agent's terminal, run:

hermes model

Pick the provider you have a subscription with and follow the login flow it gives you (it will hand you a link to open in your browser).

2. Turn off the compaction alert

Hermes shows a recurring warning that gets in the way while you work. Turn it off once now. Run this in the terminal, or paste it to the agent in chat:

hermes config set compression.codex_gpt55_autoraise false

3. Prove the whole loop works

In your backchannel, ask it to actually do something:

Paste to your agent

Look up today's most interesting AI news story and give me two sentences on why it matters.
