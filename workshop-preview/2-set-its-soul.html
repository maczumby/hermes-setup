Part 2

Set its job and personality

Your agent has a file called its soul that holds who it is: what it's for, and how it should talk to you. Right now that file is empty. You fill it by describing the agent you want, in your own words.

We do this now, after you've picked the model, on purpose. The soul conversation runs on your subscription tokens from part 1, so writing it costs you nothing beyond what you already pay.

The soul isn't the same as the standing instructions you'll meet in part 3. The soul is who the agent is. Standing instructions are how it behaves in shared channels. Keep the soul to who it is and what it's for, and leave the operating details out.

1. Tell it to set its soul

In your backchannel, paste this and then keep typing in your own words:

Paste to your agent, then describe the agent you want

Let's set your soul. Here's who I want you to be:

You exist to [what it's for — e.g. keep my calendar and email from
slipping, and draft replies that sound like me].

Talk to me like [how — e.g. a sharp assistant: direct, brief, plain
language, no filler].

You are not [what it should stay out of — e.g. a replacement for my own
judgment on anything that matters].

Say "soul" the way you'd say body and soul. That word is how Hermes knows you mean its identity file, not a passing instruction. You can rewrite it any time, so don't overthink the first pass.

2. Keep the soul about identity, not plumbing

Agents tend to stuff operating details into the soul: file paths, tool rules, which integration lives where. That belongs elsewhere. If your agent starts doing it, tell it to pull that out. A soul that stays about who the agent is stays readable, and it's easier to hand to someone else later if this agent ends up working alongside other people.
