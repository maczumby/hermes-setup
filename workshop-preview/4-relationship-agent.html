Part 4 — your personal CRM agent

Your network, remembered

Your network is your business, and right now it lives in a CSV you've never opened and a memory that leaks. In the next hour your agent gets a copy it can actually reason over — and by tomorrow it will owe you one real follow-up. Order matters: calendar first (your agent already sees it), then LinkedIn, then we lock it down, and only then do you decide about email.

1. Start your LinkedIn export, then install the tool

First, a browser errand whose result arrives by email in about ten minutes — start it now so it's ready when we need it. If you did this in the pre-work, skip to the paste. Open Get a copy of your data, pick the top option, the larger archive (that's the one with your connections in it), and submit. When LinkedIn's email arrives, download the zip. Don't unzip it.

Paste to your agent — in your backchannel (raw file)

Read this and set yourself up with it:
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/maczumby/network-observatory/main/skills/network-observatory/SKILL.md

This is going to become my personal relationship memory. Don't ask me for
any files yet — my LinkedIn export is still on its way. When you're set up,
tell me in one short paragraph what this tool will let me do, then stop
and wait for me.

2. Calendar first — the quarter you already lived

While LinkedIn prepares your export, use something your agent can already see. You upload nothing and grant nothing new in this step — and notice that the agent asks before it remembers a single new person. That's the pattern for everything today.

Paste to your agent (raw file)

Before we touch LinkedIn: you can already see my calendar. Look at my
meetings from the last 90 days and add them to my new relationship memory.

Rules: who and when only — never store meeting titles, descriptions, or
locations. Skip anything with more than about 10 people (webinars aren't
relationships), and skip anything that isn't a real person. Before you
create a record for anyone new, show me the list of names and let me
confirm who's worth tracking — I'll say yes or no per person.

When you're done, tell me three things: how many meetings you found, how
many people that covers, and the three people I've met with most. Then stop.

3. The map — and the moment two lives connect

Your export should have arrived by now. This step builds the star map, then something better: your agent notices that people from your calendar might be people from your LinkedIn, and asks you — one at a time, reversibly — whether they're the same person.

Paste to your agent — with your LinkedIn zip attached, still zipped (raw file)

Here's my LinkedIn export. Import it, build my map, and get me a link I can
open in my browser. Then read me the summary in plain language: how many
people, over how many years, my top companies.

After that, check whether any of the calendar people you already know match
my LinkedIn connections. Show me each match one at a time as a plain
question — I'll confirm or reject each one, and I know every confirmed
match can be undone later.

When my map link works, offer me three questions I could ask about my
network. If you offer to password-protect the link, tell me "not yet" —
we're doing that together in a few minutes.

Now make it yours. Pick a company you actually want in the door at this quarter, and fill in the blank:

Paste to your agent — fill in the blank first (raw file)

Who do I know at ________? For the best two or three, tell me who they are
and, if you know, when we were last actually in touch.

4. Lock it — security you can touch

Your map link works right now, and it's public: anyone with the URL sees your network. Feel the difference between trusting a promise and trusting a lock. Pick a real password (your password manager works here — it's a normal login page, not a browser popup) and send this in your backchannel only, never in a shared channel:

Paste to your agent — with your password filled in (raw file)

Lock my map link with a password. Set it to: ____________
Confirm the lock is actually working before you tell me it's done, then
send me the link again.

5. The email decision — an honest fork

You already have a working CRM: calendar plus LinkedIn is real. Email makes the warmth picture much deeper, because most professional touch happens in email — but it's the one step today that grants new access, so here's the full price tag. The connection can only ever see who you emailed and when — never subjects, never bodies, never attachments. Google's servers enforce that, not a promise. The costs while the app is in Google's testing program: an "unverified app" warning (expected — you'll see it), your Gmail must be on Mari's tester list first, and Google asks you to reconnect once a week (one click; nothing is lost in between). Yes next month works exactly as well as yes today.

If yes: paste the block below. Your agent sends you to a Connect page; enter your Gmail there, then copy the command the page shows and paste it back to your agent before anything else — it's shown only once. Then approve the Google screen. One quirk: after your agent runs the command, start a fresh chat before testing — the connection appears in the next session.

Paste to your agent — only if you're opting in (raw file)

I want to add the email connection we discussed — the one that can only
ever see who I emailed and when, never the messages themselves. Walk me
through it one step at a time, starting with the Connect page link, and
wait for me at each step. If Google tells me "access denied," tell me
exactly what to send Mari.

Once the connection tests clean, set the sweep running — it works in the background while we finish:

Paste to your agent (raw file)

Now go through my email history and add it to my relationship memory —
who wrote to whom and when, nothing else. Skip newsletters, robots, and
anything that isn't a person; when you can't tell, ask me instead of
guessing. Afterwards, show me any new identity matches one at a time,
build my warmth table and send me the link, and answer one question:
who have I gone cold on that I probably shouldn't have?

If no (or not yet): you sit out nothing. Send this instead:

Paste to your agent — the no-email path, equally real

Build my warmth table from what you have so far and send me the link.
Then: who should I reach out to this week, and why?

6. One real follow-up, then tell the room

Don't invent one. Think of the follow-up that's been sitting in the back of your head this whole session — the intro you promised, the deck you owe, the person you keep meaning to circle back to. That one. Fill in all three blanks:

Paste to your agent (raw file)

Remind me to follow up with ________ about ________ by ________.
Then read the reminder back to me so I know it stuck, and tell me how
I'll hear about it when it's due. After that, one last question:
who else have I left hanging?

Last move: post one line in #network-wins — the shape of your win, not your notes. A first name or a role is plenty:

First win: my agent reminded me I owe ___ a ___ — due ___.
First surprise: it knew ___ that I'd forgotten.

And the standing ritual: every Monday, ask your agent "what's on my radar this week?" When it surfaces something good, post it here. A room of operators whose agents each know one network — this channel is where those networks start finding each other.

7. Later, at home: let your networks talk

There's a second act when you're ready: give your agent rules for what it may say about your network when other people ask in shared channels ("does she know anyone in climate tech?"), then put a few agents in one channel and watch several networks become visible without anyone handing over their data. The paste that sets the rules — including everything your agent may NOT share — is here. Do it after the workshop, read the rules before you send them, and tighten anything that makes you wince.

8. Eight words you and your agent now share

  • Map — your LinkedIn connections drawn as a star field you can pan, search, and click.
  • Trellis — your agent's relationship memory: who people are, when you last talked, what you owe them.
  • Warmth — how recently you've truly been in touch, always shown with the date that proves it; "no data" means unmeasured, never cold.
  • Receipts — the dated evidence behind every claim; if the agent says "warm," it can show the meeting or email date behind the word.
  • Loop — a follow-up you've promised, with a due date; it resurfaces on its own.
  • Radar — a short list of people worth reaching out to right now, each with a reason; it stays quiet when there's nothing real.
  • Mute — hide a non-person (a newsletter, a robot) from your memory; unhide any time.
  • Merge — confirming two records are the same person; recorded, and undoable with one sentence.
